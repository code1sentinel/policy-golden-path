Writing control statements

The parts of a control statement

Review privileged and payment access at least every [90] days to remove access that is no longer needed.

  • Action first: what must be done, not who does it.
  • Scope: exactly what it applies to.
  • Limit: how often, how fast, or when; a [parameter] where the organisation sets the value.
  • Purpose: why, or the risk it treats.

Tools (Okta, Veritas, Symantec) belong in guidance, or with how the control is implemented, so the control outlives them.

A practice counts as adopted when it scores or more with no outstanding area for improvement, and partly adopted from . Scores describe how a statement is written, not whether it is true.

AI drafting

Off

Off unless you turn it on. Codify still drafts with rules. With AI on, you can also ask a model to draft a clause's controls, with your own API key or with Ollama on this machine (no key). Every AI draft is scored like any other, marked as drafted by AI, and stays a draft until you review it.

What is sent. One clause per request: its text, its section heading and Codify's rule drafts for it. It goes straight from this browser to the provider you choose, under your account and that provider's terms. It does not pass through Codify, and your key stays in this browser.
Do not send classified or sensitive text. Use AI drafting only for policy text your organisation allows you to send to this provider. If in doubt, leave it off: the rule drafts need no AI.

Codify is open source (MIT) from the GRC Engineering Club Singapore. Control statements follow the style of Singapore's IM8 Reform catalog; this tool is not affiliated with GovTech.
